Framework · The portfolio layer

Strategy, investment and finance: one portfolio, one set of decisions.

Strategic Portfolio Management is how an organisation makes sure its capital goes where its strategy says it should, at a pace its balance sheet can carry, and stops where the evidence says it must.

The problem

Three plans, one pot of capital

In most organisations, strategy, investment and finance each run their own plan. Strategy sets ambitions. Investment builds cases one at a time. Finance builds a budget around what has already been approved.

Nobody owns the whole portfolio. So capital follows whichever case is presented best, the funding envelope is discovered late, and projects that no longer fit the strategy keep running because stopping them is no one's job.

StrategySets direction, rarely sets capital limits
InvestmentJudges each case alone, not against the others
FinanceFunds what's approved, finds the limit late

The four disciplines

What strategic portfolio management looks like in practice

01

One set of priorities

Strategic objectives are turned into explicit capital priorities, so every case can be judged against them.

02

One comparable pipeline

Every investment case is measured the same way: strategic fit, return, peak equity, liquidity and risk. Cases compete with each other, not just with a hurdle rate.

03

One capital envelope

The financial plan sets how much, how fast and on what funding, before cases are approved, not after.

04

One review rhythm

A regular portfolio review where strategy, investment and finance decide together what to start, continue, re-scope or stop.

Questions for your next Board meeting

  1. Who owns the whole portfolio, rather than each project in it?
  2. Can we compare our largest investment cases on the same measures, side by side?
  3. Do we know our capital envelope before we approve the next case, or only after?
  4. When did we last stop a project because it no longer fitted the strategy?
