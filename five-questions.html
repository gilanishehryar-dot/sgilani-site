Five questions before any investment case reaches the Board

I've taken more than 25 investment cases to Boards and Management Committees. These are the five questions that most often separate a case that can be defended from one that only looks good.

  1. 1

    Who owns the five assumptions that move the return most?

    Not which team: which person. If the answer takes more than a minute, the case is not ready. An unowned assumption is an unpriced risk.

  2. 2

    What would have to be true for this to lose money, and how close are we to that?

    Ask for the break-even on price, cost and timing, and the gap between it and the base case. A case that only shows the upside has not been tested.

  3. 3

    What happens if it is a year late?

    Timing moves returns more than most Boards expect. Ask for the effect of a 12-month delay on the internal rate of return, peak funding and covenant headroom.

  4. 4

    What does it displace?

    Capital spent here is not spent elsewhere. Ask how the case compares with the rest of the pipeline on the same measures, and whether it fits the capital envelope the Board has already set.

  5. 5

    What will tell us it is going wrong, and who can stop it?

    Agree the early warning signs, the review dates and the point at which the decision comes back to the Board. Approval without stop rules is a one-way door.

None of these questions needs a new model. They need the existing one to be governed: owned assumptions, a clear decision boundary, a model that can be interrogated and a regular review. That is what the Decision Integrity System sets out.
