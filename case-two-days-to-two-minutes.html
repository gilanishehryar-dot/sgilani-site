Case study · Strategic Finance + Governance

From two days to two minutes: rebuilding a corporate plan as an operating system

A master developer's plan took two days to re-run, so the leadership team debated last month's numbers. The rebuild let them test the question in the room.

2 minplan re-run time, down from 2 days
OrganisationPublic Investment Fund-backed master developer, Khafji, Saudi Arabia
AudienceManagement Committee and Board
ScopeCorporate financial plan for a multi-phase, mixed-use master development

01 · The situation

A plan too slow to steer by

The corporate financial plan was a static, consultant-built model. Each re-run took two days, so by the time a scenario came back the question had usually moved on. Assumptions changed without a clear owner, and leadership discussions centred on the last version rather than the next decision.

02 · What I did

Rebuilt the plan as a governed system, not a bigger spreadsheet

  • Rebuilt the corporate model top-down, modular and auditable, cutting run-time complexity.
  • Added an assumptions register: every critical input tiered, owned and logged, with material changes escalated.
  • Linked it to a risk register and a scenario engine, so timing, pricing, cost and funding could be stress-tested on demand.
  • Put a monthly business review on top, so the model drives the monthly executive conversation instead of reporting the past.

03 · The decision it enabled

Bringing Phase 2 forward, tested in the room

With re-runs in two minutes, the Management Committee could see the effect of a change before it decided, not two days after.

Development phasing

The Committee was able to assess the financial implications of bringing forward Phase 2 versus retaining the original development programme, including the impact on peak equity requirements, cash flow and overall project returns. The Management Committee agreed with my recommendation.

What other Boards can take from this

Speed without governance just produces wrong answers faster. The gain came from pairing a faster model with owned assumptions and a monthly rhythm, so the leadership team could trust the number it was looking at.
